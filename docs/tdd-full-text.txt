1. Executive Summary and Product Definition
The platform converts MegaScript Digital’s professional website-building methods into a repeatable, self-service product. The customer
describes their business through a guided conversational interface; an orchestration service plans a supported site; multiple focused AI
jobs draft content; a composition engine combines registered components, themes and curated assets; and the customer previews, edits,
pays for, publishes and manages the result in one account.
1.1 Product statement
Working proposition: “A professional website for your business, built and managed through a simple conversation.” The customer should
not need to understand hosting, DNS, server deployments, React, CSS, sitemap generation or image optimisation to launch a site. The
product should make informed defaults, ask only questions that materially affect the result, and expose advanced controls only when
helpful.
1.2 Core capabilities
 Conversational intake with Google OAuth or email/password signup, email verification and optional phone verification.
 Generation of a multipage business website: Home, About, Services/Products, Blog and Contact, with industry-specific additions
where appropriate.
 A modular registry of professionally engineered section components, each versioned and defined by content schemas, layout rules,
responsive behaviour, media slots and animation presets.
 Multi-stage AI generation with a site-plan stage, page-level content stages, separately generated launch blog drafts, schema
validation and targeted retries.
 A curated Cloudinary-backed image catalogue with application-level metadata, licensing records, responsive transformations and
fallbacks.
 Hosted publishing using a shared multi-tenant renderer, domain mapping, TLS, SEO artefacts and versioned release/rollback.
 Portable static export of pages and local assets, plus an explicit dependency report for functionality that requires a backend.
 Monthly and annual plans with a defined AI allowance, separately visible domain registration/renewal charges and supported top-ups
or upgrades.
 Brevo-powered transactional email, notifications, renewal reminders and opt-in platform newsletters.
 Mobile-first dashboard: desktop sidebar, mobile bottom navigation, a top notification control, Home, Build, Websites and Profile
destinations.

1.4 Core operating principle
CANONICAL SOURCE OF TRUTH Persist structured site configuration and content revisions. Do not make generated HTML or
a transient model response the only record of the website. Hosted rendering and portable export must both be deterministic projections
of an approved configuration version.
2. Goals, Non-Goals and Product Principles
The MVP should solve the common case exceptionally well: a credible business website with good content, robust mobile layouts, SEO
foundations and reliable publishing. It should not attempt to recreate every feature of a bespoke agency project on day one.
2.1 Goals
 Make professional multipage websites usable by nontechnical business owners.
 Turn the owner’s business facts into coherent text using section-specific instructions, not arbitrary AI-generated source code.
 Create controlled visual variety through section variants, supported themes, curated imagery and weighted selection.
 Keep creation, editing, hosting, domains, billing and customer communications in one managed interface.
 Allow the customer to export a standard static site and use another host, while transparently identifying dynamic dependencies.
 Support future growth in industries, sections, integrations, plan types and AI providers without redesigning the platform.
 Use mobile-first design and accessibility as engineering constraints, not final polish.
2.2 Non-goals for initial release
 Unlimited arbitrary code generation or an unrestricted visual canvas.
 A full ecommerce engine with carts, inventory, payments, shipping and tax workflows.
 Complex booking systems, portals, memberships or custom business logic in the initial MVP.
 Guaranteed Google rankings, indexed pages, leads or revenue.
 Cross-site similarity scans during every composition; diversity is handled by deterministic rules and within-site validation.
 Automatic synchronisation of an exported website unless the customer has connected a supported external deployment.
 Customer-managed API keys for AI models. The platform manages approved provider credentials server-side.
 Running customer-specific application deployments by default; the first architecture is shared-renderer/multi-tenant.

3.2 Primary journey: first website
1. Visitor opens product marketing site and understands core value, pricing and domain terms.
2. Visitor creates an account with Google or email/password. Email verification completes before publication and domain registration
are enabled.
3. User enters Home dashboard and selects Build.
4. Conversational intake records business name, industry, location/market, services, audience, preferred style, logo/brand colours if
supplied, contact links and required compliance details.
5. Planner creates a sitemap and proposed composition from registered sections; only high-impact unknowns trigger follow-up
questions.
6. Parallel or dependency-aware jobs generate page content and three initial blog drafts (draft status by default).
7. Asset matcher resolves curated Cloudinary assets for each manifest media slot.
8. Renderer creates preview on a platform subdomain; automated checks run.
9. User requests specific changes through chat, approves the preview and selects a plan/domain.
10. Payment verification triggers publishing/domain workflow; the system reports verified stages.
11. Website goes live; renewal reminders, usage display, backup and monitoring continue.
3.3 Return editing journey
A returning user selects a website, enters Build/Edit, and makes a natural-language request. The request router classifies the change as
content patch, asset replacement, supported style token update, section insert/remove/reorder, page addition or unsupported/dynamic
feature request. A scoped job updates only affected configuration, produces a preview revision, validates dependencies and asks for
approval where the change has material impact. Publishing never happens silently for a destructive or high-impact change unless the
owner has explicitly configured auto-publish.

4. Functional Scope and MVP Boundary
The scope prioritises a reliable core product over the largest possible feature list. Each release should be independently useful and should
not require unbuilt ecommerce or booking features to work.
4.1 MVP included
 User registration/login and verified email; Google sign-in if OAuth configuration is available.
 Dashboard and conversational builder responsive on mobile, tablet and desktop.
 One site per base plan initially (limit configurable), with Home, About, Services/Products, Blog and Contact pages.
 Section registry and a deliberately curated starter set of variants; do not ship hundreds before the quality rubric is proven.
 The standard site chrome: sticky header, responsive navigation, four-column desktop footer collapsing gracefully on smaller screens,
floating WhatsApp contact control if supplied, contact forms and CTA patterns.
 Theme tokens, logo upload/reference, palette/font options, image catalogue selection, reduced-motion-safe GSAP presets.
 Staged AI jobs to plan, draft page content, draft three blog posts, validate and build previews.
 Hosted publishing on platform subdomain and a first custom-domain connection path.
 Static export of content pages and assets, with dependency report and README.
 Monthly/annual plan records, payment checkout integration, usage ledger and domain renewal records.
 Transactional email and in-app notifications; opt-in newsletter.
 Administrative functions for users, sites, assets, section metadata, prompt versions, jobs, billing status and audit events.
4.2 Later phases
 Connected Git/third-party deployment and automated push publishing.
 React/TypeScript source export with package lock/build instructions.
 Search Console authorisation and analytics dashboards.
 Multiple websites per account, agency workspaces and collaborator access.
 Dedicated e-commerce, booking, membership, multilingual and advanced forms modules.
 Template marketplace and third-party component certification.
 Automated A/B test variants, traffic-based performance suggestions and other optimisation features.
4.3 Product limits should be data-driven
Page limits, AI allowances, number of sites, export entitlement and hosting quotas should be represented in plan configuration rather than
scattered conditionals. Every limit needs a user-visible explanation, an API-side enforcement check and an administrative override path
with an audit trail. Plan enforcement must not break access to account data, invoices, domain transfer information or export pathways
where the customer is entitled to them.
5. System Context and Logical Architecture

deploying every domain concept as a separate microservice until workload or team size proves that split is worthwhile.

5.3 Deployment topology
The preferred first topology is one Next.js application plus durable background jobs and managed PostgreSQL. The same application
serves dashboard routes and public customer domains, resolved by hostname to a site ID. Domain hosting provider choice remains
provisional until traffic profiles, custom-domain counts, commercial terms and export strategy have been modelled. A separate static
export worker may use a controlled build sandbox rather than running arbitrary customer-supplied code in the web request process.
5.4 Trust boundaries
 Untrusted browser input enters through authenticated, schema-validated endpoints.
 Model output is untrusted until schema, content policy, asset ID and component ID validation succeeds.
 Webhook requests are untrusted until signature/authenticity checks pass and idempotency is applied.
 Public custom-domain requests must resolve only to verified domain mappings; unknown hosts do not default to a customer tenant.
 Admin operations and provider credentials remain behind server-side access boundaries.
 Static exports are artifacts: dynamic external service dependencies must be disclosed rather than silently assumed.

7. Multi-Tenant Site Model and Isolation
Each customer site is logically independent even though public pages share a renderer and runtime. Tenant isolation is a security
requirement and must be enforced at both service and database boundaries where feasible.
7.1 Tenant resolution
12. Parse and normalise the HTTP Host header through trusted framework handling; reject malformed, unexpected or untrusted
forwarded-host values.
13. Resolve hostname to a verified DomainMapping or approved platform subdomain. Unknown hostnames route to a safe not-found
page, never to a guessed tenant.
14. Resolve the canonical site ID and site publication pointer using an allowlisted host mapping.
15. Read only the published version of the site, its page content and asset references; do not expose draft content to public requests.
16. Cache by canonical site ID plus published version. Purge or rotate keys on publication and domain transfer.
7.2 Isolation invariants
 Every user-owned query includes a tenant/site ownership predicate or uses a verified access scope object.
 A `siteId` supplied by the browser is not proof of ownership.
 Domain ownership verification occurs before mapping is activated.
 Asset write credentials and provider API keys are server-only; browser upload flows use short-lived, scoped upload signatures if
direct upload is supported.
 Support/admin cross-tenant access requires explicit privileged permission and must be logged.
 Background jobs carry tenant/site identity, minimal payload and an authenticated internal job ID.
 Tenant IDs are included in logs and metrics only where this does not leak customer-sensitive content.

8. Website Configuration and Composition Engine
The composition engine builds a valid site plan from business goals, selected theme and the registered section catalogue. The engine is not
a cross-site similarity detector. It relies on local constraints, weighted selection, stable randomness and human-tested design rules.
8.1 Canonical site configuration
The canonical site configuration is a versioned structured document. It describes metadata, theme tokens, page routes, section instances in
order, content payload references, asset bindings, navigation, footer settings, features and export dependencies. The configuration does not
embed untrusted arbitrary source code. It references registered component IDs and versions.
8.2 Composition algorithm
17. Classify business type and primary conversion goal (enquiry, call, visit, request quote, portfolio view, subscription or other supported
goal).
18. Select a compatible global visual direction based on explicit user choices and safe defaults.
19. Choose the baseline sitemap; include mandatory core pages and add industry-relevant optional pages only when supported.
20. For each page, obtain candidate sections from the registry by page type, industry tags, component requirements, theme compatibility
and feature flags.
21. Apply constraints: hero first; minimum/maximum section counts; avoid repeated visual patterns on the same page; require
CTA/contact paths; respect media availability; do not duplicate the same section variant adjacent to itself.
22. Use weighted deterministic selection seeded by site ID plus composition version. Avoid costly global comparisons against all other
sites.
23. Resolve content schemas and media slots for each selected section, then enqueue page generation jobs.
24. Validate the overall plan, required routes, section IDs/versions and layout rules before generation proceeds.
25. Persist plan version and selected component manifest for reproducibility, review and rollback.

37. Verify the acting user owns the site and the plan/site is eligible to publish.

35. Operations, Backup and Disaster Recovery

 Canonical site configuration, page content and release metadata.
 Prompt and manifest version history stored in source control or durable registry.

actual database, hosting and backup plan.

39.4 Event handling rules
Events are versioned, persisted when delivery reliability matters, and idempotently consumed. Event consumers must tolerate duplicates
and reordered delivery. Never place payment credentials, auth tokens, full prompt transcripts or unnecessary personal information in event
payloads. A durable event record should contain enough to retry work without reconstructing state from a browser session.

42. Runbooks, Checklists and Glossary
42.1 Release checklist
 No unresolved critical security or data isolation failures.
 Prompt/manifest schemas validate and required component versions are present.
 All required public routes pass link, metadata, mobile and accessibility checks.
 Hosted publish and rollback are tested against a nonproduction site.
 Static export passes external-host smoke test and includes dependency report.
 Payment webhook replay and duplicate registrar response tests pass.
 Cloudinary production assets have licence/source metadata and expiry review where needed.
 Brevo sender authentication, transactional templates and unsubscribe flow are tested.
 Subscription plan limits, usage unit wording and renewal disclosures are final and reflected in server enforcement.
 Backup restore and incident runbooks have named owners.
 Monitoring/alerts cover AI spend, job lag, domain/TLS errors, payment webhook lag and published-site health.
 Customer-facing privacy, terms, support and data portability paths are accessible.
42.2 Manual launch smoke test
62. Create test account, verify email, sign out/in and reset password.
63. On mobile, build a sample service-business site from short intake and confirm only essential follow-ups appear.
64. Inspect homepage, About, Services, Blog index, three draft articles and Contact at small phone/laptop/wide desktop sizes.
65. Change a homepage heading and one image only; confirm unrelated content remains unchanged.
66. Verify no invented testimonials/prices/awards; inspect alt text and contact details.
67. Publish to platform subdomain; check status, HTTPS, sitemap, robots, canonicals and indexability.
68. Attach controlled test domain and ensure pending DNS/TLS is not represented as active prematurely.
69. Run sandbox checkout and replay webhook; confirm one fulfillment and one invoice/usage update.
70. Export a static bundle, unpack it on a separate static host, click every core route and inspect dependency report.
71. Trigger a failed-generation retry and a publish rollback; ensure progress persists and prior live version remains available.
